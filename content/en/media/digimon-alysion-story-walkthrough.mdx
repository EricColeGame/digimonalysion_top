## Prep Checklist Before Your First Story Battle

Beta players consistently note that the tutorial covers the basics but leaves much of the game's depth unexplained. Treat the first hour as setup, not as the real game.

- **Read every tutorial popup once.** The memory gauge rule — that playing cards pushes memory toward your opponent and crossing to their side ends your turn — is the single concept that decides most early matches.
- **Set a 30-second habit before each match.** Check your egg/breeding slot, check your security stack count, then check what your opponent's Digimon can evolve into.
- **Don't spend currency on cosmetics early.** Card access and speed-up items pay for themselves; skins do not.
- **Play three practice matches with a starter-style list** before you commit to building anything custom.
- **Note which chapter walls you hit.** A loss is information about your curve, not about your skill.

| Turn phase | What happens | Walkthrough takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| Unsuspend | Your Digimon untap | Plan attacks around what untaps next turn |
| Draw | You draw one card | Card advantage decides long matches |
| Breeding | You act in the breeding area | Free evolution value — never skip it |
| Main | Play cards, evolve, attack | Memory management happens here |
| End | Turn passes | Track what your opponent can punish |

## Digimon Alysion Story Walkthrough, Phase by Phase

### Early chapters: learn the rhythm

Early story matches are forgiving on purpose. Use them to internalize sequencing: hatch in breeding, evolve on curve, and attack only when you can afford the memory swing. The most common beginner error is attacking with everything and handing the opponent a huge memory pool on their turn.

### Mid chapters: fix consistency first

Once opponents start running coherent evolution lines, raw power stops working. This is where most players hit their first wall in the Digimon Alysion story walkthrough. The fix is almost never "grind more cards" — it is trimming your deck down to one clear game plan with a reliable low-cost base.

| Deck role | What to prioritize | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Low-cost base | Cheap Rookies you can play without passing turn | Keeps memory on your side |
| Evolution bridge | A consistent Level 4 and Level 5 line | Prevents dead hands |
| Top end | One or two finisher-level Digimon | Closes games before decks run out |
| Interaction | Options or effects that remove threats | Answers opposing boss Digimon |
| Draw / search | Effects that find your pieces | Consistency beats raw stats |

### Later chapters: manage timers, not just battles

Community reports suggest that story completion feeds timers and speed-up items into your account. That makes the late game a scheduling problem as much as a deck-building one. Bank speed-up items for chapters you know are hard, and spend them where a loss is most likely — not on the easy rematches you already clear.

Because the game is still pre-release at the time of writing, treat any specific reward number you see online with suspicion. What holds up across beta impressions is the shape of the economy: story clears feed resources, resources feed deck quality, and deck quality unlocks the next block of story.

| Reward type | Priority | Best use |
|---|---|---|

| Currency | High | Targeted card acquisition, not random spending |
| Speed-up items | Medium-high | Hard chapters and timed events |

A simple rule keeps this manageable: if a reward does not help you clear the next chapter, it can wait. That single filter prevents most early account mistakes.

## Deck-Building Priorities That Make Story Battles Easier

Story opponents are predictable, which means you can build against them. Instead of chasing the strongest cards, build the most repeatable opening hand.

- **Cap your top end.** Two or three finishers is enough; more creates dead draws.
- **Respect the memory curve.** If half your deck costs four or more, you will pass turn constantly.
- **Pack at least two answers** to a single large opposing Digimon.
- **Test in short bursts.** Three matches is enough data to know whether a change helped.
- **Keep a written note** of which card underperformed — most players cut the wrong cards first.

For the underlying rules and card pool, the [official Digimon Card Game website](https://world.digimoncard.com/) is the most reliable reference, and it stays accurate as the digital version evolves.

## Common Mistakes and Player Experience Tips

| Mistake | What it costs you | Better habit |
|---|---|---|
| Attacking with everything | Hands the opponent a huge memory turn | Attack only when the swing is safe |
| Ignoring the breeding area | Free evolution value left on the table | Hatch and evolve every turn you can |
| Overbuilding the top end | Dead cards in the opening hand | Cap finishers at two or three |
| Spending speed-ups early | Wasted on chapters you'd clear anyway | Save them for confirmed walls |
| Rebuilding a deck after one loss | Time lost, no real data | Play three matches before changing |

Player experience also flags one softer issue: the tutorial teaches you to win, not to understand. If a mechanic feels unclear after chapter three, deliberately lose a low-stakes match while watching the memory gauge. It is the fastest way to internalize how turns actually end in Digimon Alysion.

## FAQ

**How long does a full Digimon Alysion story walkthrough take?**
No official chapter count or playtime figure has been confirmed. Based on beta structure, expect story mode to be your main activity for the first stretch of the game, with timers pacing how fast you can chain chapters together.

**Can I finish the story without spending money?**
Beta impressions suggest yes, provided you spend rewards on cards and speed-up items rather than cosmetics. Efficiency matters more than payment.

**Should I skip the tutorial?**
Don't. The tutorial is the only place the memory gauge is explained in a controlled setting, and every later match depends on it.

**What should I do after clearing the current story content?**
Polish your main deck, bank speed-up items, and practice the turn sequencing you learned. When new chapters arrive, that preparation is what lets you clear them quickly.

Follow this Digimon Alysion story walkthrough as a framework rather than a script — the chapters will change, but the underlying habits of memory discipline, deck consistency, and reward prioritization will keep working long after the story is finished.
