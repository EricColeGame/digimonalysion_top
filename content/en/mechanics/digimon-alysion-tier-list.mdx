## What a Digimon Alysion Tier List Actually Measures

A tier placement is a prediction, not a fact. It estimates how a card, a Digimon line, or a whole deck archetype will perform across a large number of games, in a specific format, against a specific field of opponents. Change any one of those three variables and the ranking should move.

Three things drive every placement:

- **Raw card power** — how much a single card does relative to what it costs you.
- **Deck context** — whether the card has partners that cover its weaknesses.
- **Pilot skill** — how much value an average player actually extracts from it.

That last one is why tier lists argue so much. A card that rewards expert timing can sit in A tier for a veteran and C tier for a newcomer, and both ratings can be correct.

| Ranking target | What you are really measuring | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Individual cards | Efficiency per play | Does this card do enough on its own? |
| Digimon lines and evolution paths | Payoff versus setup cost | Is the payoff worth the investment? |
| Deck archetypes | Consistency and matchup spread | Does the plan survive disruption? |
| Matchups | Win-rate gap between two decks | Which popular deck does this lose to? |

Ranking archetypes is almost always more useful than ranking single cards. A mediocre card inside a coherent plan beats a powerful card with no support, and tier lists that ignore this end up recommending cards nobody can actually build around.

## The Tier Framework: S Through D, Defined

Before you argue about placements, agree on what the letters mean. Most tier list disagreements are definition fights in disguise. Here is a framework that holds up across card games and translates cleanly to Digimon Alysion.

| Tier | Definition | Signs a card or deck belongs | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S** | Format-defining; warps what everyone else plays | High efficiency, few bad matchups, shows up everywhere | Bans, restrictions, or hard counters arriving |
| **A** | Strong and flexible; safe competitive pick | Reliable plan, answers to common threats | Slightly slower or less consistent than S |
| **B** | Solid but meta-dependent | Great against one popular strategy, weak against another | Falls off if the field shifts |
| **C** | Niche or tech choice | Fixes a specific problem very well | Dead weight in the wrong matchup |
| **D** | Avoid for now | High setup cost, low payoff, or no support | Wastes deck slots and testing time |

Most players should build from A and B tier first. S-tier strategies attract mirror matches and targeted counter-building, which means your win rate can drop even though your deck did not get worse. A and B decks often post better results simply because opponents are not prepared for them.

## The Five Criteria Behind Every Ranking

If you want your own Digimon Alysion tier list to hold up, score every candidate on the same five criteria. The weights below are a starting point — adjust them once you know whether your local field is fast or grindy.

| Criterion | Weight | What to look for | Red flags |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consistency | 25% | Does the plan come together in most games? | Needs two or three specific cards every time |
| Tempo | 20% | Can it pressure or stabilize early? | Slow start with no catch-up mechanism |
| Card and resource advantage | 20% | Does it trade up over time? | Every play is a one-for-one or worse |
| Flexibility | 20% | Can it adapt to control or aggro? | One linear line with no pivot |
| Skill floor | 15% | How much value does an average pilot get? | Only rewards near-perfect sequencing |

Score each card or archetype from 1 to 5 on every criterion, multiply by the weight, and add the results. A card scoring 4, 3, 4, 4, 3 lands at a weighted 3.6 — comfortably A tier under most scales. This sounds mechanical, but it forces you to justify placements instead of repeating what a video told you.

A few practical scoring tips:

- **Test at the same skill level.** If you are new, judge cards by what you can execute, not by what a top player can.
- **Separate ceiling from floor.** Note the best-case outcome and the average outcome. Tier lists usually describe the average.
- **Reward redundancy.** Decks with multiple copies of the same effect tend to climb tiers as the format matures.

## How to Read Community Tier Lists Without Getting Burned

Community rankings are valuable, but they are a blend of real testing, personal bias, and content incentives. Treat them as evidence, not gospel. Community reports from the closed beta test are especially useful for early impressions of pacing and deck flow, but sample sizes were small and the card pool has changed since.

| Source type | Strength | Weakness | How to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Video tier lists | Fast overview, shows gameplay context | Edited highlights, creator bias | Watch for reasoning, not just placements |
| Discord polls and tier makers | Broad early impressions | Popularity masquerades as power | Spot consensus trends, then verify |
| Tournament results (when available) | Real win data against real fields | Small samples, pilot skill skew | Weight top finishes, check deck counts |
| Your own match log | Matches your skill and card pool | Tiny sample, personal blind spots | Use it as the tiebreaker |

A good rule of thumb: if a placement appears in three independent sources and you can explain *why* it works, trust it. If it appears once with no explanation, file it as player experience and move on.

## Build Your Own Digimon Alysion Tier List in Six Steps

This is the process that produces a tier list you can defend. It takes roughly a week of casual play and one focused evening of analysis.

| Step | Action | Output | Time needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Define your format and field | A written scope: casual ladder, competitive, or specific event | 10 minutes |
| 2 | Log 20 matches with one deck | Win/loss record plus notes on what decided each game | 3–4 hours |
| 3 | Score candidates on the five criteria | A weighted score for every card or archetype you saw | 1 hour |
| 4 | Cluster into archetypes | Groups of cards that share a game plan | 45 minutes |
| 5 | Place tiers by score bands | S, A, B, C, D assignments | 30 minutes |
| 6 | Re-test and revise | Updated list after the next balance change or set release | Ongoing |

Two habits make this work. First, write down *why* you lost, not just that you lost — "flooded on high-cost plays" is actionable, "bad luck" is not. Second, revisit the list after every balance change. A tier list that never moves is a tier list nobody is testing.

## Where Tier Lists Break: Patches, Sample Size, and Skill Gaps

Every tier list has an expiration date. Knowing what invalidates one helps you avoid following stale advice.

| Change | Effect on the tier list | How to respond |
|---|---|---|
| New card set or balance patch | Can move an archetype two tiers overnight | Re-score affected cards, ignore pre-patch rankings |
| Field shifts toward aggro or control | Punishes slow or greedy decks | Recheck your matchup table |
| Rising popularity of a deck | Invites counter-building | Expect its win rate to dip even if unchanged |
| Small sample sizes | Overrates lucky decks | Wait for repeat results before promoting |
| Skill gap between testers | Distorts card value | Compare data from players at your level |

The first two weeks after any change are the noisiest period in any card game. Early community rankings during that window are useful for spotting new strategies, but they are a poor basis for spending resources. Give the format a couple of weeks, then build.

## FAQ

**Is there an official Digimon Alysion tier list from Bandai?**
No. Publishers rarely release official rankings, and Bandai has not published one. Every Digimon Alysion tier list you see is community-made, which is exactly why the criteria in this guide matter more than any single chart.

**Which tier should a new player build first?**
Start with A or B tier archetypes. They tend to be more forgiving, less contested in mirror matches, and cheaper to assemble than S-tier strategies that attract counter-building.

**How often should a tier list be updated?**
After every balance change, card set release, or major shift in what people are playing. Between those events, a list can stay stable for weeks.

**Can I trust tier lists if I only play casually?**
Yes, with adjustments. Casual play has different pacing and different opponents, so weight consistency and skill floor higher than raw ceiling when you read a Digimon Alysion tier list built for competitive play.

**Do tier lists apply to every deck archetype equally?**
No. Aggressive and control strategies are rated on different curves — aggro cares about speed and reach, control cares about resource trading. Compare decks within the same plan before comparing across tiers.
