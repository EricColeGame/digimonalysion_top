## How to Upgrade Your Digimon Alysion Starter Deck

The good news is that a 17-type deck is easy to upgrade because you can see exactly what it's missing. Work through it in this order.

| Step | Action | Why It Helps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Play 5–10 games with the deck unchanged | You can't fix what you haven't diagnosed |
| 2 | Note which cards you're always happy to draw | Those are your deck's real engine |
| 3 | Note which cards sit in hand unused | These are your cut candidates |
| 4 | Open the included BT-27 pack and set aside anything relevant | Free upgrade material before you spend |
| 5 | Buy singles that support your most-drawn cards | Targeted upgrades beat random packs |
| 6 | Trim to the tightest 50-card core, then test again | Most starter decks ship slightly overbuilt |

A few upgrade principles specific to this kind of product:

- **Don't break the playsets.** The deck's consistency comes from four-ofs. Cutting one copy of a key card to fit a shiny new Rare usually makes the deck worse, not better.
- **Upgrade the Super Rare slots first.** Adding support around your three Super Rares gives a bigger return than adding a fourth unrelated threat.
- **Keep the promo in.** One-of promos in starter decks are often deliberately powerful; test before you cut it.
- **Buy singles, not boxes, once you know what you need.** The included booster pack is a nice sample, but chasing specific cards through sealed product is the most expensive way to upgrade.
- **Respect the deck's identity.** A starter deck built around Yukinamon wants cards that support that plan. Splashing an unrelated strategy usually produces a deck that does two things badly.

Finally, keep the playsheets and index cards with the deck. If you later upgrade the main deck, the original 54 cards plus the paper components make an excellent teaching deck for a friend — and that's how a $19.99 purchase ends up getting used for years.
