## Digimon Alysion Beginner Guide: Decoding the Battle Screen

If you have played the paper Digimon Card Game, the screen will look familiar. If you have not, here is the mental model that makes everything else click.

### Memory is a shared resource, not your resource

The memory gauge sits between you and your opponent. Every card you play costs memory, and the gauge slides toward whoever is "ahead." Spend past your allowance and the gauge tips to the opponent's side — your turn ends immediately and they inherit the leftover memory. This is why beginners lose: they spend everything, hand the opponent a huge turn, and then wonder what happened. Play one or two cards, leave the gauge near zero, pass.

### Digivolution is a stack, not a collection

You do not discard a Digimon to evolve it. You place the next level *on top* of it, and the cards underneath stay in the stack, often feeding inherited effects to the Digimon above. A tall stack is usually a better stack than three scattered small Digimon.

### Breeding rewards patience

Digi-Eggs hatch and grow in a separate breeding area, outside the main battle line, before moving out when you are ready. It is a slow, low-risk engine — and it is how you fill out the Digidex over time.

| Concept | Plain-English explanation | Why beginners should care |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Memory gauge | Shared pool both players spend from | Overspending gifts the opponent a free turn |
| Digivolution | Stacking a higher level on a Digimon | Inherited effects make stacks stronger than singles |
| Security stack | Your shield; chip it down to win | Attacks win games, not big boards |
| Breeding area | Side zone where eggs hatch and grow | Free value every single turn |
| Digidex | 100+ entries to complete | Long-term collection goal, not a day-one sprint |

If you want to study the underlying rules before launch, the [official Digimon Card Game site](https://worlddigimontcg.com/) publishes the full rulebook and card database — and since Alysion runs on those rules, time spent there transfers directly.

## How to Build Your First Deck Without Wasting Cards

The Digimon Card Game uses a 50-card main deck plus a small Digi-Egg deck, and you should expect Alysion to follow the same skeleton. The trap for new players is building a "greatest hits" pile of every rare they own. Resist it. A focused deck with one clear plan beats a pile of strong cards every time.

| Deck slot | Rough starting count | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digi-Egg deck | Small fixed set | Fuels the breeding area |
| Low-level Digimon | Roughly 12–16 | Cheap bodies that start stacks |
| Mid-level Digimon | Roughly 12–16 | Your main digivolution line |
| Top-end Digimon | Roughly 6–10 | Finishers that close games |
| Tamer cards | Roughly 4–8 | Ongoing memory and support effects |
| Option cards | Roughly 6–10 | Removal and surprise plays |

Three habits make the difference:

1. **Pick one line and commit.** Choose a single Digimon family or strategy and build every slot to support it.
2. **Watch your curve.** If half your deck costs six or more memory, you will pass turn constantly and lose on tempo.
3. **Let daily packs fill gaps.** Do not burn premium currency chasing a card the free pack will eventually hand you.

## Daily Habits and DigiGem Discipline

The free daily pack is the most important button in the app. Open it every day, even on days you do not play a match — it is the cheapest path to a real collection, and skipping it compounds into a weaker deck a month later.

Treat DigiGem like a budget line, not an emergency fund. The store lists bundles from small entry sets up to premium packs, and the pull rates on any card game mean early spending rarely delivers exactly what you need. Priority order for a new player:

- **Free daily pack first** — always claim it before buying anything
- **Breeding and Digidex progress** — free, permanent, and it teaches you the card pool
- **Targeted purchases only** after you know which deck you are building
- **Cosmetics and Alt-Arts last** — gorgeous, but they do not win games

One honest note: the listing discloses loot boxes and in-app purchases, and the age rating is 9+. If a younger player is involved, set spending limits before the first session, not after.
